# Venemask Cinematic Art Direction

Source of truth: `references/01-multi-mask-room-and-product-showcase.jpeg` and `references/02-venetian-hero-wide-space.jpeg`. Inspect pixels before proposing set dressing. These are conceptual reference images, not source meshes or verified product catalog.

## Visual principles
- A **single inhabitable spatial composition**, not a sequence of isolated backgrounds/cards. Camera reveals adjacent physical areas; architecture, surface and illumination continuity matter.
- 4–5 verified masks in the first frame, each grounded on physically plausible surfaces, not overlapping or stuffed into corners. Foreground/midground/background intervals, silhouettes, reflections, actual material contact.
- Compose with generous dark negative space for titles/CTA and breathing room near products. More candles, arches, fog or glitter do NOT equal quality. Subtract until focus hierarchy is unmistakable.
- Warm key light and restrained cool fill, physically plausible bounce and shadow. Make the mask visually dominant by light and depth, not oversized cards or a white halo.
- Camera/lens/DoF must follow spatial distance, not generic CSS blur. Preserve legibility and the product's true geometry and finish.
- Home transitions do not "fade to a new website". Retime the camera, light exposure and focus to move through a consistent architectural world.
- Fashion-editorial restraint: use real Venemask branding, logo, typography and language, not fictional luxury badges or generated review claims.
- Subworld differentiation: Venetian = antique gold/ivory, stone; Myth/Fantasy = natural organic tones; LoL = controlled cool blue/arcane lights, not generic neon sci-fi; Slipknot = graphite/low theatrical contrast, not a Venetian ballroom. Shared typography, spacing, button family and materials keep the brand consistent.

## Mandatory review before every handoff
Create side-by-side source-vs-output views at matched viewport/crop. Inspect silhouette, product occupancy, atmosphere depth, spatial continuity, negative space, typography, physical light, and no added clutter. Describe top 3 visual problems and fix them before saying "done". Never equate code validation with cinematic quality.
